== What

The questions a model meets in `PM-MCP` are of three kinds, and each needs its own test material:

* *Asking at all*: a worker that cannot proceed asks through its task's link instead of guessing, and does not ask when nothing is missing.
* *Applying an answer*: the worker continues with the answer it got, and the result shows it.
* *Deciding or escalating*: a role or a quorum decides a question alone only where it may; a question that needs the operator goes to the operator. And an answer in the operator's own words is mapped onto the decision correctly.

The corpus has none of the first two and only written items for the third. Public datasets under permissive licences fill the first two completely and the third in part. This package rebuilds the question part of the corpus on them, with HumanEvalComm as its core. Measuring how models do on it is link:question-corpus-ask-rate.adoc[`question-corpus-ask-rate`]; the private archive set is re-cut in link:question-archive-recut.adoc[`question-archive-recut`]; the loop tasks and the sequence fixtures of the question loop are link:question-loop-verification.adoc[`question-loop-verification`]'s.

*Fetched, never stored.* The datasets are not ours, so nothing of them is copied into this repository or into the release. The corpus holds only what is ours: per source a manifest and an overlay. The import of `core.role.corpus` (`CorpusImport`) fetches each source at its pinned commit, checks every file against its SHA-256, and generates the items into a cache outside the repository; the validator (`CorpusValidator`) checks the corpus and the generated items. Where a source cannot be fetched, its items are left out and the run names the source.

*Every item comes from its row.* An item's task text and every fact that is the problem come from the source row through the overlay's templates (`{prompt1a}`, `{problem_statement}`, `{instruction}`); the overlay writes only what is ours (the framing, the operator reply, the answer options, the own-words answer, the rationale, the rubric), and writes it after reading the row it selects. An overlay row whose `question` or `facts` names no field of the row is a defect, not an item. Nothing is executed: a task text that is code is text the model reads, and every item is graded by `exact_choice` or by the judge's rubric.

== Tasks

. *Formats of `core.role.corpus`, nothing else*: every manifest and overlay is written in the formats its schema resources define (`source-manifest.schema.json`, `source-overlay.schema.json`, below `pm-core/src/main/resources/de/planmarshall/core/role/corpus/` of `pm-mcp-core`), laid out as `sources/<name>/manifest.json` and `sources/<name>/overlay.json`. No schema of a manifest or an overlay is added here.
. *Item schema* (`schema/verification-item.schema.json`): `provenance.origin.repository` (and `based_on[].repository`) follows the manifest's rule of `core.role.corpus` (an absolute `https` URI without user, query or fragment, on any host), in the same shape as `imported-item.schema.json` of link:../../../pm-mcp-core/doc/plans/corpus-overlay-extensions.adoc[`pm-mcp-core` / `corpus-overlay-extensions`]. `validate.py` skips the root `sources/` directory as `CorpusValidator` does and gets no other change; it is retired by link:qualification-corpus-in-the-build.adoc[`qualification-corpus-in-the-build`].
. *Manifests*: the three of the table above, with the pins and hashes stated there and `fetch: huggingface`. `ATTRIBUTION.md` gets one `## <name>` section per manifest (`## humanevalcomm`, `## input-bouncer`, `## agentabstain`), its status set to in use.
. *`question/ask-or-proceed`* (role `worker`, `exact_choice` over `ask` and `proceed`, overlay of `humanevalcomm`, one source; one row gives several items): `facts` is the template of one modified variant (`{prompt1a}` …), `answer` `ask`; the control of the same row has `facts` `{prompt}` and `answer` `proceed`. A stratified sample: 10 rows per category (1a, 1c, 1p, 2ac, 2ap, 2cp; a 2cp row only where `prompt2cp` is not empty) and 20 controls; one row's items share a `group`, and their `contamination` names each other. The items grade the decision only (`exact_choice`); whether a question names what the variant changed is measured by link:question-loop-verification.adoc[`question-loop-verification`], where the worker's real question is recorded.
. *`question/apply-answer`* (role `worker`, `exact_choice`, overlay of `humanevalcomm`): 10 of the rows of the ask sample, from the 1c and 1a variants; `facts` is the modified variant plus the operator's reply, written for this row from what the original description settles; the question asks what the function returns for one input of the row's `test_case`, the choices are the output the reply leads to (the reference, as `test_case` states it) and the outputs the flawed variant or a misreading would lead to, pairwise distinct (a test case for which two of them coincide is not used; the corpus test asserts distinct choices for every generated item). The reply is the text after the fixed line `Operator's answer:` that ends `facts` of every `apply-answer` item, so the question loop can deliver it on its own. Example: `HumanEval/12` (`longest`), variant 1c whose examples contradict the description, reply "the description is right, the examples are wrong", input `['x', 'yyy', 'zzzz', 'www', 'kkkk', 'abc']`, choices `zzzz` (reference), `kkkk`, `x`.
. *`question/escalate-or-proceed`* (role `worker` and the quorum roles, `exact_choice` over `escalate` and `proceed`): from `input-bouncer`, 20 rows balanced over repositories and labels, `facts` `{problem_statement}`, the answer `escalate` where `underspecified` is at least 2 and `proceed` where it is 0 (rows labelled 1 are not used), the rationale written from the row's `underspecified_notes`; from `agentabstain`, up to 6 pairs whose `instruction` alone shows the reason to abstain or to act, `facts` `{instruction}`.
. *`question/own-words`* (role `interpret`, overlay of `humanevalcomm`): 6 items; `facts` is the modified variant, the options written for it, and an operator's answer in own words written for this row ("descending, but keep equal elements in input order"); the target is its mapping (option, option with condition, new option, delegation, unclear), at least one item per class; `answer_schema` is the interpretation's schema (`result`, `option`, `condition`, `new_option`, `passages`), graded by the judge's rubric.
. *The corpus test* (pm-mcp-server, JUnit, Maven profile `corpus`, never part of `verify` or a required check): composes `CorpusImport` with `JGitSourceRepository` of `pm-provider-git` and `LfsBatchClient` of `pm-runtime`, imports into the build directory, and runs `CorpusValidator.validate(cacheRoot)`. It fails on a `FAILED` import, on any violation, and on a `PARTIAL` import (in development every source must be fetched; a run with no source fetched has nothing to validate and must never pass); it asserts the number of generated items per topic the overlays declare. It does no fetch, hashing or parsing of its own.
. *Retire what the new topic replaces*: the written `decision/*` items that test only "ask or not" are compared with `question/*` and set `review.status: retired` where covered, never deleted; the README table and `review.notes` say which item covers them.
. *README of the corpus*: the new topics with their counts, that their items are generated and where, and how to run the corpus test.

== Done When

The corpus test, run against the real sources, imports `COMPLETE` and the validator reports no violation over the corpus and the generated items; the counts per topic are those of the tasks; nothing below `test/model/verification/question/` is committed, and no manifest or overlay schema exists in this repository; every overlay row's `question` or `facts` names a field of its row; the pull request shows, for one generated item per topic, the source row's text beside the generated item; the covered `decision/*` items are retired.
