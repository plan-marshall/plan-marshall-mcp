== What

The questions a model meets in `PM-MCP` are of three kinds, and each needs its own test material:

* *Asking at all*: a worker that cannot proceed asks through its task's link instead of guessing, and does not ask when nothing is missing.
* *Applying an answer*: the worker continues with the answer it got, and the result shows it.
* *Deciding or escalating*: a role or a quorum decides a question alone only where it may; a question that needs the operator goes to the operator. And an answer in the operator's own words is mapped onto the decision correctly.

The corpus has none of the first two and only written items for the third. The archive of real operator questions (`cuioss/plan-marshall-telemetry`, `verification/`) cannot fill them: those questions need the whole context of their plan, and they stay private. Public datasets under permissive licences can fill the first two completely and the third in part. This package rebuilds the question part of the corpus on them, with HumanEvalComm as its core.

*Fetched, never stored.* The datasets are not ours, so nothing of them is copied into this repository or into the release. The corpus holds only what is ours: a manifest per source (its repository, a pinned commit, the files with their SHA-256, its licence) and the overlays (which rows, the framing of each item, the operator replies and own-words answers written for them, the rubrics, the review status). A loader fetches each source at its pinned commit, checks every file against its hash, and generates the items into a cache outside the repository. Development here runs the loader before `validate.py` and before every measurement; a user's `pm-operator roles qualify` does the same through the product's git client. Where a source cannot be fetched or does not verify, its items are left out and the run says which source is missing: the corpus's own items are always there, so a user can then qualify on a subset only, which is accepted.

Considered and not used: HiL-Bench (`ScaleAI/hil-bench`: no licence on the dataset or its repository, so no right to use; its design, a blocker registry with a resolution per blocker, is taken over as an idea only), Ambig-SWE (the repository `Ruan-WH/Ambig-SWE` carries MIT with data, but cannot be confirmed as the authors' release; it is added only once its origin is confirmed), Dialogue-SWEBench and the underspecified SWE-bench variant of "Ask or Assume?" (no data release found).

*Licences*: MIT and Apache-2.0 both allow commercial use, change, and redistribution, MIT under its copyright and permission notice, Apache-2.0 under its licence text, any `NOTICE`, and a statement of changes. Since nothing is redistributed, those conditions are not triggered; `ATTRIBUTION.md` still names every source, its licence, its holders, and the content it carries from elsewhere (HumanEval inside HumanEvalComm; the SWE-bench data and the GitHub issue texts inside BouncerBench), so that what a run fetches and under which terms is on record. A dataset licence covers what its authors hold; content they took from elsewhere keeps its own terms.

*How each is fetched* (measured 2026-10-14): HumanEvalComm and AgentAbstain are plain git repositories (`HumanEvalComm_v2.csv`, 0.6 MB); the BouncerBench files are Git LFS objects (`input-bouncer/test.csv` 33 MB, `bouncerbench-lite/test.csv` 19 MB): git fetches their pointers, and the loader fetches each object from the repository's LFS endpoint and checks it against the SHA-256 the pointer names.

== Tasks

. *Source manifests* (`sources/<source>.json`): the repository URL, the pinned commit, every file used with its SHA-256 (for an LFS file the pointer's object id and size), the licence (SPDX id and the URL of its text), the holders, and the third-party content it carries. A source is updated only by a reviewed change of its pin; the review reads what changed upstream.
. *Overlays* (`sources/overlay/<topic>.json`): our part per topic, keyed by the source's row identifier (`task_id`/`name` of HumanEvalComm with its variant, BouncerBench's instance id): the item framing, the operator replies and own-words answers written for them, rubric items, the controls chosen, and each item's review status. An overlay entry never repeats the source's text.
. *Loader* `load.py` (stdlib Python and the git CLI, like `validate.py`): reads the manifests, fetches each source at its pin into a cache outside the repository (`$XDG_CACHE_HOME/pm-mcp/corpus-sources/<source>/<commit>/`, reused while it verifies), fetches LFS objects from the repository's LFS batch endpoint, checks every hash, and generates the items of every overlay into a generated corpus directory beside the cache; `validate.py` then validates the corpus's own items together with the generated ones. A source that cannot be fetched or does not verify is reported by name and its items are skipped; the exit code tells a complete run from a partial one. Generated items carry `provenance.kind: imported` with `origin` naming the source, its commit, and the row.
. *The product side* uses the same manifests and overlays: `pm-operator roles qualify` fetches through the product's git client (JGit, with its LFS support or the same batch endpoint) into the machine's cache and generates the items there; a qualification on a partial corpus records which sources were missing, and a role is qualified only on what was graded. The shipped corpus (link:qualification-corpus-in-the-build.adoc[`qualification-corpus-in-the-build`]) is the corpus's own items, the manifests, and the overlays, never generated items.
. *Schema*: a grading method `execution` (the answer is code; the tests run without network in a confined job, pass when every test passes), `provenance.kind: imported` with a required `origin.license` (SPDX id), and the schemas of the manifest and the overlay; `validate.py` checks them, that every manifest has its section in `ATTRIBUTION.md`, and that no third-party text is committed below the corpus.
. *`question/ask-or-proceed`* (role `worker`, overlay over HumanEvalComm): per problem chosen, the modified description as a task and the closed choice `ask` or `proceed`; the original description of the same problem as its control (`proceed`). A stratified sample: 10 problems per category (1a, 1c, 1p, 2ac, 2ap, 2cp) and 20 controls, chosen so that one problem's variants never share a warm session (`group`, `contamination`). A rubric item checks that the question names what was changed (HumanEvalComm's measure of a good question).
. *`question/apply-answer`* (role `worker`, grading `execution`): the same modified problems, with the clarification the original description gives as the answer, written as an operator's reply; the solution passes the HumanEval tests. This is the objective check of "the worker applied the answer" that no archive item can give.
. *`question/escalate-or-proceed`* (role `worker` and the quorum roles): BouncerBench input-bouncer issues with their labels (too vague: escalate; fixable: proceed), a sample balanced over repositories and labels; and the AgentAbstain pairs that can be stated without their sandbox.
. *`question/own-words`* (role `interpret`): written answers in own words to HumanEvalComm clarifications ("descending, but keep equal elements in input order"), with their correct mapping (option, option with condition, new option, delegation, unclear), `provenance.kind: written`, based on the imported problem.
. *The question-loop tasks*: each `apply-answer` problem as a minimal repository generated by the loader (the function stub, the tests, a task that names the file), which the measurement link:question-loop-verification.adoc[`question-loop-verification`] uses for Q1 to Q5 and R3: asking is forced, the answer to deliver is given, and the tests show whether it was applied. R1, R2, and R4 (continuation against starting again) need real work before the ask and keep the scenarios from the private archive.
. *The private archive set is re-cut*, in `cuioss/plan-marshall-telemetry` (`verification/`), not here: its question items become escalate-or-decide items (reference `escalate` where the record shows the decision needed the operator; the operator's actual choice kept in the notes, not as the target), because a decider without the plan's whole context cannot be graded on the operator's choice; its own-words and scenario files stay.
. *Retire what the new topic replaces*: the written `decision/*` items that test only "ask or not" are compared with `question/*` and retired where covered (`review.status: retired`, never deleted).
. *Contamination*: HumanEval is in the training data of most models, which may recall the original and proceed instead of asking. The run of the package measures the ask rate on the modified problems against the controls per model, and records it in the corpus README; a category whose ask rate does not differ from its controls is reported, not silently kept.

== Done When

`load.py` fetches and verifies every source at its pin and generates the items; `validate.py` passes over the corpus's own and the generated items; a run with one source unreachable completes on the subset and names the source; no third-party text is committed; every generated item names its source commit and row; the ask rate per category against the controls is recorded; the private archive set is re-cut; `question-loop-verification` builds its loop tasks from `question/apply-answer`.
